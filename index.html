Marble Acid Reaction Experiment

Effect of Acid Concentration on Reaction Rate

Experiment Overview

This experiment investigates how different concentrations of acetic acid (vinegar) affect the reaction rate with marble chips (calcium carbonate). Three groups are set: control group with distilled water, dilute acid group with 20% vinegar and concentrated acid group with 100% vinegar.

Experimental Procedure

Step 1: Prepare the Beakers

  • Wash and dry the three 100 mL beakers thoroughly.
  • Label them clearly: 1 (Control), 2 (Dilute Acid), 3 (Concentrated Acid).

Step 2: Measure the Marble

  • Use an electronic balance to weigh approximately 20 grams of marble chips.
  • Put the marble chips into Beaker 1, 2 and 3 respectively.

Weigh marble with balance

Step 3: Prepare the Solutions

  • Beaker 1 (Control): 80 mL distilled water
  • Beaker 2 (20% Vinegar Solution): 16 mL vinegar + 64 mL distilled water
  • Beaker 3 (100% Vinegar Solution): 80 mL pure white vinegar

Measure distilled water

Measure pure vinegar

Mix solution evenly

Step 4: Set Up the Experiment

  • Place all three beakers on a flat desktop without moving them casually.
  • Keep all samples under identical ambient temperature and environment conditions.
  • Stir each mixture gently with a clean glass rod to make sure marble chips are fully immersed in liquid.

Arrange beakers on desktop

Pour solution into beakers

Stir the mixture gently

Step 5: Observe and Record

  • Note down initial phenomena such as bubble generation and surface change of marble right after preparation.
  • Observe and record experimental variations at fixed time intervals. Record the bubble producing speed, surface condition of marble and liquid state changes.
  • After the experiment finishes, separate the liquid from solid marble, rinse and dry the chips, then weigh to check mass variation.
